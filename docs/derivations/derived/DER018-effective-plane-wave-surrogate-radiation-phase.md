# DER018 — Effective plane-wave surrogate and radiation-phase origin of ponderomotive incidence

Status: derived

## Setup

GammaForge uses a narrow-line, first-harmonic description of radiation from
ultrarelativistic electrons crossing a bounded laser pulse. The laser need not be a
global plane wave. Nevertheless, when the electron is very relativistic, the field is
relativistically weak enough that the electron is only weakly deflected, and the optical
wavevector sampled along the trajectory varies slowly, it is useful to replace the
actual field *along that trajectory* by a temporary plane-wave surrogate.

The surrogate is introduced only to obtain plane-wave electron kinematics and a simple
radiation phase. It is not an assertion that the focused field is globally a plane wave.

Use \(c=1\) in the intermediate four-vector formulas. Let

- \(p^\mu=m(\gamma,\mathbf u)\), with \(\mathbf u=\mathbf p/m\) and
  \(\gamma=\sqrt{1+\mathbf u^2}\);
- \(\mathbf n_0\) be the fixed effective laser propagation direction;
- \(\mathbf N\) be the photon observation direction;
- \(\ell^\mu=(1,\mathbf n_0)\), so \(\ell^2=0\);
- \(\phi=\omega_L\,\ell\cdot x\) be the effective laser phase.

To avoid collision with xigma's photon spectral coordinate \(s\), define the
plane-wave light-front momentum

$$
\kappa=\ell\cdot u=\gamma-\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf u.
$$

The GammaForge regime of interest is \(\gamma\gg1\), weak trajectory deflection,
\(a_0<1\) for the unresolved first-harmonic model, and observation within the
near-electron radiation cone used by the existing emission kernel. The plane-wave
kinematic identities below are more general; those restrictions enter when reducing
them to the production resonance.

## 1. Criterion for one exact equivalent plane wave

For a plane wave,

$$
F_{\rm pw}^{\mu\nu}(\phi)
=
k^\mu A'^\nu(\phi)-k^\nu A'^\mu(\phi),
\qquad
k^2=0,
\qquad
k\cdot A'=0.
$$

The Lorentz equation implies

$$
\frac{d}{d\tau}(k\cdot p)
=
q\,k_\mu F_{\rm pw}^{\mu\nu}u_\nu
=
0.
$$

Hence a known Lorentz-force trajectory can be generated by one fixed plane wave only if
there exists a fixed null direction \(k^\mu\propto(1,\mathbf n_0)\) such that

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\gamma-\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf u=\mathrm{const}}
$$

over the whole trajectory.

In three-vector language, with the actual mechanical force
\(\mathbf f=d\mathbf p/dt\), the same condition is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf f=\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf f}.
$$

If a fixed \(\mathbf n_0\) satisfies this identity, define

$$
\mathbf f_\perp
=
\mathbf f-\mathbf n_0(\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf f),
$$

$$
\mathbf E_{\rm eff}
=
\frac{\mathbf f_\perp}
{q(1-\mathbf n_0\cdot\boldsymbol\beta)},
\qquad
\mathbf B_{\rm eff}
=
\mathbf n_0\times\mathbf E_{\rm eff}
$$

in \(c=1\) units. Then along the known trajectory

$$
q(\mathbf E_{\rm eff}+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B_{\rm eff})
=
\mathbf f.
$$

Thus the force equality can be exact along the trajectory even though the constructed
field is only defined through the phase history sampled by that trajectory.

A generic focused-field trajectory will not satisfy constant \(\kappa\) exactly. The
surrogate approximation is that \(\kappa\) varies negligibly over the part of the
trajectory that contributes appreciably to the radiation. The most relevant diagnostic
for radiation is ultimately the accumulated phase error, not only
\(\Delta\kappa/\kappa\).

The trajectory fixes the effective null *direction* but not the overall normalization of
\(k^\mu\). The physical laser carrier, or the encountered carrier phase for a chirped
pulse, supplies that normalization.

## 2. Plane-wave momentum as a function of phase

Resolve the dimensionless momentum into components parallel and transverse to
\(\mathbf n_0\):

$$
\mathbf u=u_\parallel\mathbf n_0+\mathbf u_\perp.
$$

For the surrogate plane wave, \(\kappa=\gamma-u_\parallel\) is constant. Together with
the mass-shell condition,

$$
\gamma^2=1+u_\parallel^2+u_\perp^2,
$$

this gives

$$
\boxed{
\gamma(\phi)
=
\frac{1+\kappa^2+u_\perp^2(\phi)}{2\kappa}
},
$$

$$
\boxed{
u_\parallel(\phi)
=
\frac{1-\kappa^2+u_\perp^2(\phi)}{2\kappa}
}.
$$

Transverse canonical momentum is conserved. Define the dimensionless transverse
potential difference by the sign convention

$$
\mathbf a(\phi)
\equiv
\mathbf u_{\perp0}-\mathbf u_\perp(\phi),
$$

so

$$
\mathbf u_\perp(\phi)=\mathbf u_{\perp0}-\mathbf a(\phi).
$$

Therefore

$$
u_\perp^2
=
u_{\perp0}^2
-2\mathbf u_{\perp0}\cdot\mathbf a
+a^2.
$$

The linear term oscillates with the carrier and has zero cycle mean for the usual
zero-DC plane-wave pulse. The \(a^2\) term has a nonzero mean and produces a secular
longitudinal drift.

## 3. Radiation phase in an arbitrary observation direction

For radiation of frequency \(\omega'\) detected along \(\mathbf N\),

$$
\Psi(t)
=
\omega'\left[t-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf x(t)\right].
$$

Since

$$
\frac{d\phi}{dt}
=
\omega_L(1-\mathbf n_0\cdot\boldsymbol\beta)
=
\omega_L\frac{\kappa}{\gamma},
$$

the phase derivative is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\Psi}{d\phi}
=
\frac{\omega'}{\omega_L}
\frac{\gamma-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u}{\kappa}
}.
$$

Define

$$
\mu=\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0,
\qquad
\mathbf N_\perp=\mathbf N-\mu\mathbf n_0.
$$

Because \(u_\parallel=\gamma-\kappa\),

$$
\gamma-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u
=
(1-\mu)\gamma+\mu\kappa-\mathbf N_\perp\cdot\mathbf u_\perp.
$$

Substituting the plane-wave expression for \(\gamma\) gives

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\Psi}{d\phi}
=
\frac{\omega'}{\omega_L}
\left[
\frac{1+\mu}{2}
+
\frac{1-\mu}{2\kappa^2}(1+u_\perp^2)
-
\frac{\mathbf N_\perp\cdot\mathbf u_\perp}{\kappa}
\right].
}
$$

This equation is exact for the surrogate plane wave.

With \(\mathbf u_\perp=\mathbf u_{\perp0}-\mathbf a\),

$$
\frac{d\Psi}{d\phi}
=
\frac{\omega'}{\omega_L}
\left[
C_0+\mathbf C_1\cdot\mathbf a(\phi)+C_2a^2(\phi)
\right],
$$

where

$$
C_0
=
\frac{1+\mu}{2}
+
\frac{1-\mu}{2\kappa^2}(1+u_{\perp0}^2)
-
\frac{\mathbf N_\perp\cdot\mathbf u_{\perp0}}{\kappa},
$$

$$
\mathbf C_1
=
-\frac{1-\mu}{\kappa^2}\mathbf u_{\perp0}
+
\frac{\mathbf N_\perp}{\kappa},
$$

$$
\boxed{
C_2=\frac{1-\mu}{2\kappa^2}.
}
$$

After integration,

$$
\Psi(\phi)
=
\frac{\omega'}{\omega_L}
\left[
C_0\phi
+
\mathbf C_1\cdot\int^\phi\mathbf a(\varphi)\,d\varphi
+
C_2\int^\phi a^2(\varphi)\,d\varphi
\right]
+\mathrm{const}.
$$

The key result is that the secular nonlinear phase is proportional to

$$
\boxed{1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0}.
$$

The observation dependence of the ponderomotive redshift is therefore a direct
consequence of how the laser-driven longitudinal displacement enters the detected
radiation phase.

## 4. Cycle-averaged line centre

Let \(\rho\) denote the scalar cycle-averaged nonlinear strength entering the
plane-wave line centre. Averaging the oscillatory term linear in \(\mathbf a\) gives

$$
\left\langle
\frac{\gamma-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u}{\kappa}
\right\rangle
=
\frac{\gamma_0-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u_0}{\kappa}
+
\frac{\rho}{2\kappa^2}
(1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0),
$$

where \(p_0^\mu=m(\gamma_0,\mathbf u_0)\) denotes the corresponding unperturbed/drift
momentum used to label the electron.

For the first harmonic, the secular phase advance gives

$$
\boxed{
\omega_R(\mathbf N)
=
\omega_L
\frac{\kappa}
{\gamma_0-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u_0
 +\dfrac{\rho}{2\kappa}(1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0)}
}.
$$

The same denominator can be packaged into the cycle-averaged quasi-momentum

$$
q^\mu
=
p_0^\mu
+
\frac{m\rho}{2\kappa}\,\ell^\mu,
$$

for which

$$
q^2=m^2(1+\rho),
\qquad
\ell\cdot q=\ell\cdot p_0=m\kappa.
$$

Then the line centre is simply

$$
\omega_R
=
\omega_L
\frac{\ell\cdot q/m}
{n_N\cdot q/m},
\qquad
n_N^\mu=(1,\mathbf N).
$$

This quasi-momentum form is a repackaging of the radiation-phase result; it is useful for
understanding the information content of possible table coordinates.

## 5. Ultra-relativistic reduction and recovery of DER015

Let \(\mathbf e\) be the electron direction and \(r\) the small angle between
\(\mathbf e\) and \(\mathbf N\). In the ballistic \(\beta=1\) production reduction,

$$
F=1-\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf n_0,
\qquad
\kappa\simeq\gamma F.
$$

For \(\gamma\gg1\) and \(r\ll1\),

$$
\gamma-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf u
\simeq
\frac{1+\gamma^2r^2}{2\gamma}.
$$

Insert both approximations into the cycle-averaged line centre and multiply numerator
and denominator by \(2\gamma\):

$$
\boxed{
\omega_R
\simeq
\frac{2\omega_L\gamma^2F}
{1+\gamma^2r^2+
 \rho\,\dfrac{1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0}{F}}
}.
$$

Thus the nonlinear incidence factor is

$$
\boxed{
Q
=
\frac{1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0}
     {1-\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf n_0}
}.
$$

This is the factor implemented by DER015. DER018 derives its geometric origin; it does
not redefine GammaForge's trajectory-averaged nonlinear scalar. Production obtains raw
\(\hat a\) from DER003/DER016 and uses

$$
\rho\longrightarrow\hat a
$$

at the nominal line-centre level, so the denominator becomes
\(1+Q\hat a+\gamma^2r^2\).

With DER013's reference-incidence normalization
\(D=F/F_0\) and DER016's trajectory-averaged encountered carrier \(\bar C\), this is
exactly the production structure

$$
s_R
=
\frac{D\bar C\,\gamma^2}
{1+Q\hat a+\gamma^2r^2}.
$$

## 6. Spatial chirp and phase normalization

For a laser phase

$$
\Phi_L
=
\omega_0(t-\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf r)
+
\delta\Phi,
$$

the frequency encountered along a ballistic trajectory is

$$
\frac{d\Phi_L}{dt}
=
\omega_0F\,C(t),
$$

with

$$
C(t)
=
1+
\frac{\partial_t\delta\Phi+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\delta\Phi}
{\omega_0F}.
$$

Thus chirp changes the phase normalization/frequency scale, whereas \(Q\) describes the
observer projection of the nonlinear longitudinal drift. They are distinct pieces of the
same radiation-phase construction. When their trajectory variation matters, DER016 and
DER017 retain the required variance and covariance rather than replacing them by
independent uncorrelated broadening parameters.

## 7. Why quasi-momentum does not reduce the lossless table dimension

For the line position alone, \((p^\mu,\rho)\) can be repackaged into \(q^\mu\).
Normalizing \(q^\mu\) to an effective velocity appears to reduce four quantities
\((\gamma,\theta_{e,x},\theta_{e,y},\rho)\) to three.

That reduction loses the invariant norm

$$
q^2=m^2(1+\rho).
$$

Without it, the physical momentum cannot be reconstructed. If the full quasi-momentum
is retained, then

$$
\boxed{
p_0^\mu
=
q^\mu
-
\frac{q^2-m^2}{2\,k\cdot q}\,k^\mu
}
$$

and no information is lost, but \(q^\mu\) has four independent components: it merely
replaces the three physical momentum degrees of freedom plus the nonlinear strength.

This matters because xigma needs more than the line centre. The angle-resolved
radiation weight contains the physical electron momentum, for example through the
relativistic beaming factor already exposed in DER009, and through the polarization
geometry. Therefore the effective velocity sufficient to evaluate the resonance is not
sufficient to evaluate the emitted intensity.

An independently varying encountered carrier \(\bar C\) supplies one additional scalar.
There is consequently no exact dimensional reduction, from this change of variables
alone, of the current lossless table

$$
(\gamma,\theta_{e,x},\theta_{e,y},\hat a,\bar C).
$$

A lower-dimensional representation would require an additional approximation or a
demonstrated finite set of extra per-bin moments that reconstructs the discarded
physical-momentum dependence.

## 8. Freezing \(Q\) within one electron's radiation cone

A possible approximation is to replace the observation projection in the nonlinear
term by its value on the electron direction,

$$
\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf N
\longrightarrow
\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf e,
$$

which makes \(Q\to1\) for that electron.

Let \(\alpha\) be the angle between \(\mathbf e\) and \(\mathbf n_0\), and let
\(\vartheta\) be the observation angle from \(\mathbf e\), with azimuth \(\varphi\). Then

$$
\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf N
=
\cos\alpha\cos\vartheta
+
\sin\alpha\cos\varphi\sin\vartheta.
$$

For small \(\vartheta\),

$$
\boxed{
Q-1
\simeq
\frac{\tfrac12\cos\alpha\,\vartheta^2
      -\sin\alpha\cos\varphi\,\vartheta}
     {1-\cos\alpha}.
}
$$

Across the characteristic radiation cone \(\vartheta\sim1/\gamma\):

- exactly head-on incidence has no linear term, so \(\Delta Q=O(1/\gamma^2)\);
- generic oblique incidence gives \(\Delta Q=O(1/\gamma)\);
- nearly co-propagating incidence is poorly conditioned because
  \(1-\cos\alpha\simeq\alpha^2/2\), giving a leading scale
  \(O[1/(\gamma\alpha)]\).

Therefore freezing \(Q\) can be accurate for highly relativistic head-on or moderately
oblique collisions, but it is not uniformly valid. Even when it is accurate, it removes
only the observation dependence of the nonlinear *line position*. It still does not
allow \(\hat a\) to be eliminated from a lossless angle-resolved table if the physical
momentum is required for the intensity.

## Result

The effective-plane-wave construction gives three linked results:

1. A single exact plane-wave surrogate for a known trajectory requires a fixed null
   direction for which \(\kappa=\gamma-\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf u\) is constant. In the
   intended focused-pulse use, this is an approximation to be checked along the
   radiation-producing trajectory.

2. The plane-wave radiation phase contains a secular nonlinear term proportional to

   $$
   (1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0)\langle a^2\rangle,
   $$

   which reduces in the ultra-relativistic ballistic emission geometry to the verified
   DER015 factor

   $$
   Q=
   \frac{1-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf n_0}
        {1-\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf n_0}.
   $$

3. Rewriting the resonance in terms of a quasi-momentum can simplify the line-position
   algebra but does not reduce the number of independent variables required by the full
   angle-resolved intensity. The present five-dimensional xigma table therefore remains
   the lossless representation supported by this derivation.

## Validation targets

This file remains at status derived. Useful checks before promoting it are:

1. **Analytic plane wave.** Integrate an electron in a known pulsed plane wave and verify
   constant \(\kappa\), the reconstructed \(\gamma(\phi)\) and \(u_\parallel(\phi)\),
   and the radiation-phase derivative above.

2. **Recovery of \(Q\).** Extract the fundamental line centre from the full phase for
   several observation directions and incidence angles, and compare the nonlinear
   coefficient with DER015's \(Q\).

3. **Focused-pulse surrogate.** On direct Lorentz trajectories through representative
   bounded pulses, compare the exact phase
   \(\Psi_{\rm actual}=\omega'[t-\mathbf N\cdot\mathbf x(t)]\) with the surrogate phase.
   The accumulated phase error is the most relevant validity metric.

4. **Cone-freezing scaling.** Numerically verify the \(O(1/\gamma^2)\) head-on and
   \(O(1/\gamma)\) generic-oblique variation of \(Q\), and identify the failure region
   near co-propagation.

5. **Full observable.** Any future lower-dimensional table must be compared against the
   existing five-dimensional result for both line centres and directional intensities,
   not only for resonance positions.

## Open questions

- How should the fixed effective \(\mathbf n_0\) be chosen for a strongly focused pulse:
  the nominal axis, a trajectory-weighted optical phase gradient, or the direction that
  minimizes accumulated radiation-phase error?
- Over what pulse/focusing range does the constant-\(\kappa\) surrogate remain accurate
  enough for the requested detector spectral resolution?
- What is the sharp validity boundary of the unresolved first-harmonic approximation as
  \(a_0\) approaches unity and higher harmonics acquire non-negligible energy?
- Can a reduced table plus a small number of physical-momentum moments reproduce the
  angle-resolved intensity accurately enough to justify dropping \(\hat a\) as an axis?
